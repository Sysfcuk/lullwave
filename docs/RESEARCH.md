# Sound and sleep: what the evidence says, and how Lullwave follows it

Researched November 2026. This is a summary of reviews and key studies, not medical advice.

## What the research says

### Music before bed helps some people sleep better (moderate evidence)
- A Cochrane review (Jespersen et al., 2022; 13 randomised trials, 1,007 adults with insomnia) found
  **moderate-certainty evidence that listening to music improves self-reported sleep quality**. Effects on
  objective measures (time to fall asleep, sleep efficiency) were small or unclear, and no harms were reported.
  Trials typically had people listen for 25 to 60 minutes at bedtime, nightly, over several weeks.
  [Cochrane summary](https://www.cochrane.org/CD010459/music-listening-to-improve-sleep-quality) ·
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36000763/)

### Slow, soft, smooth, instrumental music works best
- A meta-analysis in older adults (Chen et al., 2021, *J Am Geriatr Soc*) found **"sedative" music**
  (slow, about 60 to 80 bpm, soft, smooth melody) improved sleep quality more than rhythmic music.
  Narrative reviews say the same: instrumental, simple structure, gentle dynamics.
  [Elements of music that improve sleep (review)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12713922/) ·
  [Frontiers in Sleep review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sleep/articles/10.3389/frsle.2025.1707162/full)

### Catchy tunes can backfire through earworms
- Scullin et al. (2021, *Psychological Science*): music at bedtime, including instrumental versions of
  familiar songs, increased **nighttime earworms**, and these were linked to worse sleep measured in the
  lab. Unfamiliar, non-repetitive music may avoid this.
  [Baylor news](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2021/music-listening-near-bedtime-disruptive-sleep-baylor-study-finds) ·
  [APS release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/2021-june-bedtime-earworms.html)

### Continuous noise: mixed evidence; pink noise looks a little better than white
- Riedy et al. (2021, *Sleep Medicine Reviews*, 38 studies) rated the evidence that continuous broadband noise
  improves sleep as **very low quality**, and warned against promoting it without better studies.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33007706/)
- Capezuti et al. (2022, *J Clin Sleep Med*, 34 studies) found positive results more often for **pink
  noise** (9 of 11 studies) than white noise (6 of 18), but no strong evidence overall, and no harms from
  short-term use. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9163611)
- The striking pink-noise results for deep sleep and memory (Ngo et al., 2013) come from **closed-loop**
  stimulation: short bursts timed to brain waves measured by EEG. A phone app can't do that.
  [Ngo et al.](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/auditory-closed-loop-stimulation-of-the-sleep-slow-oscillation-en/)

### Binaural beats: weak evidence
- Reviews describe the findings as small, inconsistent and from small studies.
  [Systematic review of binaural beats and EEG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37205669/)

### Loud and sudden sounds disturb sleep
- The WHO's night noise guidelines (2009) cite strong evidence that noise during sleep raises heart rate
  and causes **arousals** (brief shifts to lighter sleep) and awakenings; they set 40 dB as the outdoor
  night target. For infants, guidance attributed to the AAP is to keep sleep sounds **below 50 dB**,
  far from the cot. [WHO](https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289041737) ·
  [Children's National](https://riseandshine.childrensnational.org/baby-sound-machines-and-hearing-loss/)

### Screen brightness matters more than colour
- Night-shift colour filters alone did not stop melatonin suppression; **lowering brightness** did more.
  Low "melanopic" light (dim, less blue) shortened time to fall asleep.
  [LRC on Night Shift](https://everydaymatters.rpi.edu/new-lrc-study-evaluates-the-effectiveness-of-apples-ipad-night-shift-application/) ·
  [Melanopic light study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36854795/)

## How Lullwave lines up

| Evidence | Lullwave |
| --- | --- |
| Slow (about 60–80 bpm), soft, smooth, instrumental | All worlds are instrumental, between 40 and 86 bpm, and slow further through the night. |
| Sedative beats rhythmic | Beats only play early (high energy) in Night Drive, Neon Rain and Disco Cat, then drop away. |
| 25–60 minutes at bedtime | Default timer is 45 minutes; the music slows, thins and fades out over the last stretch. |
| Avoid earworms | Composed live; **changed:** Night Drive's hook and the cave's bell motif now change every phrase, and melodies only play early on. |
| Avoid sudden loud sounds | **Changed:** gentler compression of peaks; thunder, passing cars and lightning flashes now soften with the night and stop late on. |
| Pink noise has the best (still limited) noise evidence | **Added:** a pink noise layer alongside brown noise, off by default. |
| No good evidence for binaural beats | Not used. |
| Brightness matters more than colour | **Changed:** the screen dims sooner and further; warm mode also dims; visuals fade to black and the frame loop stops. |
| Low volume | **Added:** in-app tips advise "quieter than a soft conversation". A web app can't measure loudness. |
| Persistent insomnia needs treatment | Tips note it's a relaxation aid and point to a doctor and CBT for insomnia. |

## Honest limits
- Music studies mostly measured **how well people felt they slept**; objective gains are uncertain, and
  participants knew they were listening to music, so placebo effects are possible.
- Lullwave's generative music hasn't been tested in a study; it follows the features linked to benefit.
- The playful worlds (Disco Cat, Night Drive) start with more rhythm than the evidence would pick; they
  wind down to sedative music, but the calmer worlds are the better choice for someone who struggles to sleep.
