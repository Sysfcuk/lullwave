Sleeping well with sound

  • Keep it quiet. Set the volume as low as you can still hear it, quieter than a soft conversation. Loud or sudden sounds can rouse you without fully waking you.
  • Use the timer. The studies that found music helps used 25 to 60 minutes at bedtime. Lullwave slows, softens and fades out on its own, so nothing stops abruptly.
  • Make it a habit. The benefits showed up when people listened every night for a few weeks.
  • Let the screen go dark. Dimming matters more than warm colours. Let the visuals fade, and put the phone face down.
  • Noise is optional. Pink or brown noise can mask disturbing sounds, but the evidence is mixed. If you use it, keep it low.
  • No earworms. The music here is composed as it plays and never repeats a tune, so there is no catchy hook to get stuck in your head.

Lullwave is a relaxation aid, not medical treatment. If poor sleep goes on for weeks, talk to a doctor; CBT for insomnia is the recommended first treatment.
